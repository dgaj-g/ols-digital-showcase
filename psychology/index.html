Typical adult Stroop interference falls in the green band (about 100–200 ms). Reading is so automatic that almost everyone shows some interference.

Why does this happen?

Reading is automatic — by the time you've consciously decided to focus on ink colour, the word's meaning has already registered. When the word's meaning conflicts with the ink colour, your brain has to suppress the dominant response (reading the word) and choose the weaker one (naming the colour). That extra processing takes measurable time — usually 50 to 200 milliseconds. This is the Stroop interference effect, first reported by John Ridley Stroop in 1935. It's used to study selective attention, automaticity and cognitive control.
